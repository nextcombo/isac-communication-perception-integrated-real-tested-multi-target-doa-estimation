1. Question: Why is it so difficult to detect multiple targets in the DOA (Direction of Arrival) underwater at ports? In nearshore and other scenarios, Multiple ships radiate underwater noise simultaneously. The receiver receives overlapping acoustic signals. To estimate the direction of each ship (DOA). Four challenges need to be faced:
1  
Coherent interference: Signals generated by similar mechanical structures (propellers, engines) are highly correlated and subspace-based methods directly fail.
2  
Non-coherent interference: Independent noise sources such as small boats and waves, elevating the overall noise floor.
3  
Pulse noise (Alpha-stable): Underwater explosions and transient shocks can generate large amplitude spikes, far from Gaussian assumptions.
4  
Sensor Non-Orthogonal Deviation (NOD): After arraying, the vector sensors in each channel are not perfectly orthogonal. The deviation of the azimuth axis will introduce systematic estimation errors.
The premises of the traditional subspace method (MUSIC) are "Gaussian noise + independent sources + known number of sources", which are almost not met in real hydroacoustic environment.

Three enhancement techniques (strongly recommended to copy):  
Non-linear pre-filtering: "Squash" the sharp peaks of impulse noise before optimization - this is key for convergence in Alpha-stable noise;  
Adaptive annealing: The regularization strength decays exponentially with iteration, first suppressing large peak values and then gradually exposing weak targets;  
Top-K peak selection: When the spectral peaks exceed the desired number of targets, only the strongest ones are retained, suppressing the "ghost peaks" generated by Alpha-stable noise.  
After convergence, take the spatial spectrum and normalize it, and the position of the spectral peak represents the target DOA.

Summary of pitfalls and parameter recommendations (key points):
1 Do not directly optimize from raw data. The sharp peaks of alpha-stable noise can directly destroy least squares solutions, and pre-filtering nonlinearly significantly improves convergence stability;
2
Do not set it too small. Too small x-update matrices approach ill-conditioning, suggesting a diagonal load level;
3
Set theannealing lower limit well. A floor must be added to avoid thresholds approaching zero later, capturing all noise;
4
Do not save smoothing terms. NOD deviations can produce spikes on the spectrum, and TV smoothing terms (coefficients around) can significantly improve peak shapes;
5
Weight update denominators must be added. Scales prevent division by zero;
6
Use Top-K + minimum peak distance for peak detection. Pulse noise can produce "ghost peaks," first sort by energy and then by angle, not directly by thresholds.
